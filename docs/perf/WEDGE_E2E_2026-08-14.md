# The Orb wedge, end to end: measured 2026-08-14

**What the loop costs, and what it produces.** Vitals arrive → Vigil scores and
fires → the alert is persisted and is readable by the right clinician → Sage
drafts the assessment → its proposals become approvable cards → a clinician
approves one and declines another.

This is the number a physician champion and an investor will be quoted, so every
figure below is a median with its full range and its n, and the two findings that
reflect badly on the build are at the top rather than the bottom.

**22 timed runs against the real `medgemma:27b`, in three batches.**

---

## The two findings that matter most

### 1. The loop produced nothing to approve: 0 cards in 22 of 22 runs

All 18 assessments that completed were `ready`, carried all four sections, and
pinned NEWS2 correctly. **Not one proposed a single action card.**

Given a peri-arrest septic patient (NEWS2 19, SBP 78, SpO2 86% on oxygen, RR 32,
ACVPU voice, diagnosis "community acquired pneumonia"), the real 27B wrote
exactly four considerations every single time, and every one of them was advice:

> "Escalate care" · "Assess oxygenation and ventilation needs" ·
> "Check for signs of sepsis/septic shock" · "Consider fluid resuscitation"

The reasoning underneath each is sound, *"Fever, tachycardia, tachypnea,
hypotension, and altered mental status raise concern for sepsis given the
diagnosis of community-acquired pneumonia"*, but `action_type` was `null` on
every step. So no `sage_actions` card was created, no `action.proposed` was
emitted, and there was nothing on the screen to accept or reject.

**Median 4 considerations per assessment; 0 of them cardable; 22/22 runs.**

The pipeline is correct, the tests prove that a cardable step becomes a card,
clears the drug-safety gate, and can be approved or declined through the real
routes. The **model** is not committing to a named drug or a transfer. The
system prompt asks it to, and explicitly forbids naming a drug *class*; it
responds by staying at the level of "consider fluid resuscitation".

This does not make the build wrong. It makes the demo a **drafted assessment**
rather than a **drafted action**, and it means the `agreement` metric, the one
that would show whether clinicians trust Sage, cannot move at all.

### 2. Under an alert storm, 2 in 3 assessments are never written

| | A, sequential | B, 3 concurrent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Runs | 8 | 6 |
| Ready | **8 / 8** | **2 / 6** |
| Unavailable | 0 | **4, all `queue_timeout`** |
| Shed/timeout rate | 0% | **67%** |
| Alert still visible to the clinician | 8 / 8 | **6 / 6** |
| NEWS2 equalled the engine | 8 / 8 | 6 / 6 |

The alert survived every time, that is the safety-critical half and it held
completely. The *reasoning* did not. With three deteriorations in flight, only
the first of each batch got the model; the other two resolved to
`unavailable / queue_timeout`.

The mechanism is arithmetic, not luck, and the timings show it exactly:

* guideline retrieval: **0.5 s**
* generation: **46–52 s**
* the background lane serves **one stream at a time** (`llm_queue_service`,
  `max_concurrent = 1`)
* queue admission for the assessment is bounded at **25 s**
  (`ORB_SAGE_ASSESSMENT_QUEUE_WAIT`)

The four timed-out runs gave up at **25.7 s, 27.2 s, 27.3 s and 25.7 s**, 
precisely the bound. A second concurrent alert **can never** be admitted while
the first is generating, because 25 s < 46 s. An alert storm is exactly when the
assessment matters most.

| A, sequential, idle | **59.0 ms** | 55.5 to 103.2 ms | 8 |
| B, 3 concurrent | **56.3 ms** | 53.1 to 78.7 ms | 6 |
| C, suite running | **56.8 ms** | 53.7 to 104.0 ms | 8 |

Decomposed (batch A):

| Stage | Median | Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charting + NEWS2 + Relay write + dispatch (the nurse's POST) | **7.0 ms** | 5.5 to 36.0 ms |
| The registrar's read of the case room (the Relay GET) | **50.6 ms** | 47.8 to 97.7 ms |

Two things worth saying plainly:

* The **write** side is 7 ms. The alert path pays exactly one `create_task` for
  the assessment and never waits for it.
* The **read** side dominates. ~48 ms is `get_channels_for_user`, Relay's single
  access-control query, against a 430 MB database carrying soak-harness history.
  It is honest to quote the clinician-visible figure as ~57 ms rather than ~7 ms,
  because the clinician does not experience the write.

## What CPU contention does to it (batch C)

Batch C ran while the full 5,700-test server suite was executing on the same
machine. It is a crude proxy for a busy appliance, and the result is worth
recording:

* the alert number **did not move at all** (56.8 ms vs 59.0 ms median);
* time to first token stretched from 10.3 s to 11.3 s median, with **one run at
  29.6 s**;
* one assessment took **95 s** end to end, still inside the 120 s generation
  budget, but 1.7x the idle worst case.

The 120 s generation timeout has enough headroom for this. The 25 s **queue
admission** bound does not have enough for two patients.

## Correctness observed during the benchmark

| Property | Result |
| --- | --- |
| NEWS2 on the draft equalled the deterministic engine | **22 / 22** |
| Assessments carrying all four sections | 18 / 18 ready |
| Alert survived a failed or timed-out assessment | **22 / 22** |
| Fabricated citations reaching the payload | 0 |
| Assessments written on the small model | 0 |
| Action cards proposed | **0** |

Citation basis was `system_attributed` on 6 of 8 idle runs (`retrieved` and
`model_cited` once each), confidence `medium` throughout. The model rarely names
a document id; Orb attributes the retrieved guidelines it can support from the
answer text and labels them as such. That is designed behaviour and it reads
honestly on the card.

## How to read these numbers

* **The alert figure is the product and it is load-independent.** If it ever
  starts moving with model load, something is awaiting generation on the alert
  path. `tests/test_wedge_end_to_end.py` fails, loudly, by timing out, the
  moment that happens.
* **The assessment figure is a wait a clinician can see.** ~49 s is the honest
  cost of a careful 27B on this hardware. It is not a number to hide; it is a
  number to design the surface around: the alert now, the reasoning shortly.
* **The ranges are the finding.** Time to first token varied 9.9–11.2 s on an
  idle machine and 10.2–29.6 s on a busy one.
* **n is small.** 8 runs per sequential batch and 6 in the storm. The 67% storm
  loss is a real observation of a real mechanism, not a precise rate. Re-run
  `--storm 3 --runs 20` before quoting a percentage.
* **The bench patient is one clinical picture.** Every run used the same
  peri-arrest presentation. That makes the timings comparable and the 0-card
  finding narrower than it looks: it is one presentation, twenty-two times, not
  twenty-two presentations. Widening the case mix is the first thing to do
  before drawing a conclusion about the model's actionability in general.

## Reproducing

```bash
cd server
.venv/bin/python scripts/wedge_bench.py --runs 8 --label "sequential"
.venv/bin/python scripts/wedge_bench.py --runs 6 --storm 3 --label "storm"
```

No server required. The harness copies the dev database, brings the copy up to
the current migration set, seeds its own patients and staff, cleans up after
itself, and refuses to start under `ORB_DEPLOYMENT_PROFILE=prod`.
