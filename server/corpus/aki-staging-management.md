# Definition

This clearly-labelled reference summary describes the KDIGO definition and staging
of acute kidney injury (AKI). AKI is defined by any of: a rise in serum creatinine
of 26.5 micromol/L (0.3 mg/dL) or more within 48 hours; a rise in serum creatinine
to 1.5 times baseline or more, known or presumed to have occurred within the prior
7 days; or urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6 hours.

# KDIGO Staging

Stage 1: serum creatinine 1.5 to 1.9 times baseline, or an increase of 26.5
micromol/L or more, or urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6 to 12 hours.
Stage 2: serum creatinine 2.0 to 2.9 times baseline, or urine output below
0.5 mL/kg/hour for 12 hours or more. Stage 3: serum creatinine 3.0 times baseline
or more, or an increase to 353.6 micromol/L (4.0 mg/dL) or more, or initiation of
renal replacement therapy, or urine output below 0.3 mL/kg/hour for 24 hours or
more, or anuria for 12 hours or more.

# Management Principles

Identify and treat the cause. Classify as pre-renal, intrinsic renal, or
post-renal. Optimise volume status and haemodynamics; treat hypovolaemia with
balanced crystalloid and target a mean arterial pressure that restores renal
perfusion, using vasopressors if needed. Review the medication chart and stop or
dose-adjust nephrotoxins: NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors and ARBs during acute illness,
aminoglycosides, and iodinated contrast where avoidable. Exclude obstruction with
bladder catheterisation and renal tract ultrasound where post-renal AKI is
possible. Monitor for and correct hyperkalaemia, acidosis, and fluid overload.

# Indications for Renal Replacement Therapy

Consider urgent renal replacement therapy for refractory hyperkalaemia, severe
metabolic acidosis, diuretic-resistant pulmonary oedema, or uraemic complications
such as pericarditis or encephalopathy. Decisions should be made on the overall
clinical trajectory rather than a single creatinine or urea threshold, in
discussion with nephrology or critical care.
