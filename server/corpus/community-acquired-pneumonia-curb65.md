# Purpose

This is a clearly-labelled reference summary of severity assessment and empirical
management for community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) in adults, using the CURB-65
score. It supports triage and disposition decisions alongside clinical judgement
and local antimicrobial policy.

# CURB-65 Score

Award one point for each of the following: Confusion (new disorientation, an
Abbreviated Mental Test score of 8 or less); Urea greater than 7 mmol/L; Respiratory
rate of 30 per minute or higher; Blood pressure that is low (systolic below 90 mmHg
or diastolic 60 mmHg or below); and age 65 years or older. The score ranges from 0
to 5. A simplified CRB-65 omits urea for use where blood results are unavailable,
such as in the community.

# Risk and Disposition

Interpret the CURB-65 score together with clinical judgement and social factors.
A score of 0 to 1 indicates low severity and 30-day mortality under about 3 percent;
these patients are usually suitable for outpatient management. A score of 2 indicates
moderate severity with intermediate mortality; consider a short hospital stay or
closely supervised outpatient care. A score of 3 to 5 indicates high severity with
mortality rising steeply (roughly 15 to 40 percent); admit, and assess for critical
care where the score is 4 to 5 or there is organ dysfunction.

# Empirical Antibiotics

Follow local microbiology policy. For low-severity CAP, oral amoxicillin is
typically first line, with a macrolide or doxycycline alternative in penicillin
allergy. For moderate severity, a beta-lactam plus a macrolide is commonly used.
For high-severity CAP, use an intravenous beta-lactam/beta-lactamase inhibitor
plus a macrolide, and review for atypical and resistant organisms. Review at 48 to
72 hours, de-escalate to oral therapy when the patient is improving and stable, and
aim for a total course guided by response — commonly five days for uncomplicated CAP.
